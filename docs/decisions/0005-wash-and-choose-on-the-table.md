# ADR 0005: Shuffle in a vortex and choose cards on the table

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-16
- Supersedes: one-click dealing of every position from the top of the pile (Phase 03)

## Context

The owner wanted the draw to feel like a reader's ritual rather than a button: competing sites shuffle while a button is held. Dealing every position from the top of the pile with one click left the person no part in choosing.

## Decision

- **One table for everything.** The deck, its shuffle, the cards to choose from and the reveal all happen on the cloth itself; nothing opens in a separate area. The pack lies in the middle of the table; there is no separate shuffle button. Until the shuffle starts, the whole table is the deck to hold: one finger, the mouse or a pen pressed anywhere on it shuffles, over a position too (which is then not selected). A finger waits 0.1 s for a second one, so a two-finger pinch still zooms; panning waits until the shuffle is over. The pack keeps the keyboard: the space bar held on it, or Enter. Once every position has its card the rest of the deck leaves the table.
- **The table steps back while drawing.** From the first shuffle until every position has its card and every card has landed, the table is shown at 70% (`tableZoom`), so the pile, the vortex and the wheel have room; then it comes close again for the reveal. Position frames and numbers only show while arranging; in play the table shows cards alone (a focused position still shows its number for keyboard users). The classic three positions sit further apart.
- **One deck, one card size on the table.** The pile, the vortex and the wheel are the same size for every spread: the largest position size (21% of the table, the classic three's), at the current zoom (`DECK_CARD_WIDTH_CQW`). A chosen card changes to its position's size as it flies there, so the five-card cross's and the circle's cards shrink on the way. The owner asked for one pack size whichever spread is laid out; earlier the deck followed the positions' size. In the air the cards stay close to that size (65–95%, a little smaller towards the tip). The pile's edge is drawn in dark violet slices with a faint gold line, not a light edge.
- **Hold to shuffle in a vortex.** Holding the table, or the space bar on the pack, sends its cards one after another into a whirl: twenty cards over a second and a half, the first leaving one by one and the gaps shrinking until the last go almost together. Each card is drawn out of the front of the pack, square with it, to the front right at about 45° (the way the rings' near side turns, each at its own angle between about 40° and 49°), fast and slowing, by about one card width (0.25 s). From there it already circles with its ring and spirals straight out into it (1.0 s in all), so the whirl turns from the first moments. Cards never gather anywhere but the pack: shooting them towards the viewer lined them up at the bottom edge like a second pack, and shooting them up past the top edge bunched them in the top-right corner before they turned; the owner had both changed. The pack thins slice by slice until its last card has gone. There are four rings, wide at the top and narrow at the bottom, each turning faster than the one above; the widest reach past the table's edges, where cards are clipped and come back round. Every card strays from its ring's path, size and angle, and flips over every two laps to show a plain gold front (never a card's face). A card never shows its front while being drawn out: its first flip starts between half a lap and a lap and a third after it starts circling. There are no glowing streaks behind the cards and no still, twinkling points of light; the owner had both removed. The only lights are eight soft fireflies circling with the cards; they fade in 1.6 s into the hold, once the last card has left the pile, so a quick tap shows none. The positions step back meanwhile. A tap or Enter shuffles one round. The order is decided when the shuffle completes, so how long the deck was held never predicts the cards.
- **Letting go settles the cards, then the wheel opens.** However long the deck was held, the whirl stops and every card floats slowly down flat onto the middle of the table (0.75 s), the cards still in the pack sliding out with them. There they stop, with no swirl, and gather in a loose, askew heap where the wheel opens, at the bottom edge (0.42 s); the wheel, laid out out of sight meanwhile, opens out of that heap. A chosen card on the wheel slides forward a fifth of its height, so it clearly stands out. A quick tap therefore gets the whole settle too, instead of an abrupt stop with hardly a card in the air.
- **Only cheap motion.** Every animation is a transform or opacity of something painted once. Rings are ellipses traced by two swings a quarter turn apart (squashing a rotating circle made Chrome redraw it every frame: 15 fps instead of 60 on a throttled desktop). Flips are a squeeze across rather than a 3D turn, the gold front and its glow are painted once, and no flying card has a shadow (a 3D tumble with glowing cards ran at 24 fps). Cards in the air use a half-size back (`back-small.webp`).
- **Lay every remaining card on a wheel** along the bottom edge of the table, face down, in fixed slots. The cards are full size, so only the upper part of the arc shows above the edge, and a sideways drag turns the wheel on every screen. The table clips rather than scrolls, so focusing a card half below the edge never slides the table. Positions near the bottom are covered while choosing and appear again once the wheel is put away.
- **Choose by sliding forward, then pulling.** A tap slides a card a little forward with a gold edge, without covering its neighbours, so it is clear which card is chosen; pulling it forward draws it. Keyboard and assistive technology use Enter: once to choose, again to draw. Cards go to the empty positions in number order.
- **The jumper stays**, at the fixed `rare` rate and never shown: it is the card next to the chosen one, by the same rule in the domain (`jumperNeighbourIndex`) and in the fan (`jumperSlot`), so the card seen leaving is the card drawn.
- **No cutting the deck**, and no reshuffle until every position has its card; clearing the table still starts over.

## Consequences

- One pre-rendered card back is shared by every card in the air and on the wheel. Turning the wheel moves one layer and only the chosen card re-renders; `pnpm perf` measures holding, laying out, turning and pulling.
- `drawSeries` and the deal stagger are gone.
- E2E tests choose cards from the keyboard. A pointer test covers the pull itself.
